Start with the last real inquiry your team received. What did the person want? Where did it arrive? Who answered it? Then retrace that same journey on your phone. The first step you cannot complete, or cannot verify, is a better place to investigate than the total on your traffic chart.

## 1. Check what brought the visitor

Look at the search queries and landing pages together. A person looking for your opening hours, a supplier looking for your email and a buyer comparing your products can all land on the same site. Choose a page with a plausible buying question. Decide what a good visit would end with: a request for a test ride, a concierge inquiry or a conversation about a project.

## 2. Try the action on a phone

Start where the visitor lands. Read the headline, inspect the evidence and try the next step with one thumb. Notice where you need information the page never gives you. A retailer might need to show a model and price before the shopper feels ready to ask about availability.

For [Olympic Bootworks](/case-studies/olympic-bootworks#walkthrough), a rider can compare the Fantic range, inspect a model and choose a Tahoe shop for a test-ride request. That is an observable improvement to the buying path. A tap on an email link alone cannot tell the shop whether the rider sent the message or arrived for a ride.

## 3. Confirm that the inquiry can reach a person

Check with the person who actually handles website inquiries. Match a known request to its form receipt, booking record or inbox entry. If your analytics records a button click, name it a click. Count a received inquiry once and keep retries and internal checks out of the total.

## 4. Find what happens after delivery

Ask the owner or front desk to trace a handful of real inquiries. Did they have enough information to reply? Was the customer asking for something you sell? Did you agree on a next step? For a consultancy, two conversations with the right buyers may be worth more than dozens of unrelated requests.

## 5. Change one thing you can evaluate

Write down the observation, the change and the evidence you expect. For example: “Visitors reach the model page. Give them a location-specific request link. Review received requests with the shop.” Keep a record of the date, seasonal changes and any advertising changes. With little traffic, read the individual outcomes before drawing conclusions from percentages.
