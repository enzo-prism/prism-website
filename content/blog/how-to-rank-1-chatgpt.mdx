**TL;DR:** More buyers now ask ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, or Gemini for a recommendation instead of scanning ten blue links. Answer engine optimization (AEO) is the work of making your business easy for those assistants to find, trust, and quote: let their crawlers in, put a direct answer at the top of the pages that matter, earn real third-party proof, and ask the same buyer questions every month to see what changed. Nobody controls what an assistant says, so treat this as raising your odds, not buying a spot. Expect fewer clicks, from people who are further along.

## Why your Google traffic dipped (and why that's not the end)

More people now ask an assistant directly instead of clicking through Google's results. That collapses the funnel: fewer links, fewer clicks, and a short list of businesses that get named in the answer.

The good news is that the playbook is familiar. AEO keeps most of what worked in classic SEO and adds a few twists in how you write and where you earn proof.

## AEO in one sentence

> Make content that AI can **find**, **trust**, and **quote**, in the same **question → answer** shape people ask in.

## The 7-part AEO checklist (what to do this month)

### 1) Open the door for AI crawlers

Each assistant reads the web with its own crawlers, and most companies run separate bots for training, for their search index, and for fetching a page when a user asks. If you block them in `robots.txt`, the assistant can only repeat what others say about you.

```txt
# ChatGPT search and user-triggered fetches
User-agent: OAI-SearchBot
Allow: /

User-agent: ChatGPT-User
Allow: /

# Claude search and user-triggered fetches
User-agent: Claude-SearchBot
Allow: /

User-agent: Claude-User
Allow: /

# Perplexity
User-agent: PerplexityBot
Allow: /

User-agent: Perplexity-User
Allow: /
```

Training crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot) are a separate choice: blocking them keeps your pages out of future model training but does not remove you from search answers. Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode read your site through the normal Googlebot, and Copilot leans on Bing, so classic crawlability still matters. If a firewall or bot-protection service sits in front of your site, check that it lets these agents through too.

### 2) Start measuring AI referrals

Set up a segment in your analytics for visits from assistants. Referrers such as `chatgpt.com`, `perplexity.ai`, `claude.ai`, `gemini.google.com`, and `copilot.microsoft.com` are a good start, and ChatGPT often adds `utm_source=chatgpt.com` to the links it shows. Add "how did you hear about us?" to your forms and phone script as well: plenty of people read the answer and then search your name or call.

**Goal for this month:** a baseline you can compare against, even if it is small.

### 3) Reshape key pages into question → best answer blocks

Assistants lift short, self-contained statements. Convert or extend your most important pages (homepage sections, service pages, pricing pages) with scannable blocks:

- **Question (H3):** "How much does Invisalign cost in Palo Alto?"
  **Best answer (2 to 4 sentences):** direct, current, with ranges and context.
- **Question:** "Braces vs. Invisalign: what's better for adults?"
  **Best answer:** a short comparison plus when to choose which.
- **Question:** "How soon can I get an appointment?"
  **Best answer:** concrete timelines and a next step.

Write sentences that still make sense when quoted alone. "Dental implants replace missing teeth with a titanium post and a crown, and most patients finish in three to six months" survives being lifted into an answer. "As mentioned above, it depends" does not. Think FAQ+, not filler.

### 4) Give each question its own page, and start at the bottom of the funnel

One focused page per service or question is easier for an assistant to match than a sprawling page that covers everything. Split big topics into tightly scoped pages (`/teeth-whitening`, `/dental-veneers`, `/invisalign-cost`), each with one main question, a direct answer, and proof.

Build the buying-intent pages first: "who is the best X for Y", pricing, comparisons, and "is X right for me". That is where the questions that turn into customers live. Educational posts can come later.

Use what only you know. Your own numbers, case results, timelines, and photos are the details assistants cannot get anywhere else. A real line like "most of our implant patients are back to normal eating within a week" (if your records show it) beats generic advice every time. Never invent a number to fill the slot.

### 5) Keep the old rules that still matter

Answer engines still lean on web search, often running several searches behind one question and then writing a summary. Authority, clean technical SEO, fast pages, clear headings (one H1, then H2s and H3s), structured data, and credible links still help your pages get picked as inputs.

Pick a lane, too. A site that clearly covers one specialty for one kind of customer is easier to describe than one that covers everything. Our own June 2025 test (below) showed the flip side: one assistant described Prism mainly as a dental agency because that was what most of our content was about.

### 6) Earn third-party proof where models look

Assistants lean heavily on reviews and on real human discussion. For software, that means review sites like G2. For local services, it means Google reviews, industry directories, and high-quality forum threads where a clear, upvoted answer makes a specific recommendation. Mentions of your business name next to your service and city on other sites help models connect the two, even without a link.

**Dentist example:** a thoughtful reply in a local subreddit thread "Best dentist in [City] for Invisalign?" that explains the criteria, discloses who you are, and outlines costs or financing honestly is the kind of source assistants quote. Ask real patients for specific reviews ("mention the procedure and how it went") rather than star ratings alone.

### 7) Structure your data (and inventory)

If you sell items or have bookable services, expose structured details: availability, locations, hours, services, options, and prices or ranges where you publish them. Assistants favor sources that look ready to act on, because users want what's available now.

**Dentist example:** make sure each service page (e.g., "Dental Implants") states appointment lead time, insurance accepted, sedation options, on-site imaging, and financing, in the same format on every page. Keep the facts in your Google Business Profile, Yelp, and directories matching your site.

## What we saw when we asked four assistants about Prism (June 2025)

In June 2025 we gave Perplexity, Grok, Gemini, and ChatGPT the same prompt: "Tell me more about Prism, the agency for websites, apps, and designs." It was one test on one day, so treat it as a snapshot, not a ranking.

- **Perplexity** listed Prism second, behind a much larger agency with a long global track record.
- **Grok** did not mention Prism at all, and when we gave it our domain it described us mainly as a dental agency.
- **Gemini** listed Prism first, with an accurate summary.
- **ChatGPT** did not mention Prism; another company kept appearing for the same terms.

Three lessons held up: results differ a lot between assistants, so check each one; your content shapes how you get described (ours read as dental-only); and reputation built over years (reviews, mentions, links) carries weight you cannot shortcut. Run your own test in a private window so your history does not color the answer.

## A 30-day AEO sprint for busy owners

**Week 1: Foundations**

- Update `robots.txt` (and any firewall rules) so the assistant crawlers above can read your site.
- Set up an AI referrals segment and a "how did you hear about us?" question to capture a baseline.
- List the **15 questions** your ideal buyer actually asks ("Which whitening lasts longest?", "How to choose a CRM under $100/mo?").

**Week 2: Q&A core**

- Convert your **top 5 pages** (homepage, top 3 services, pricing) to include question → best answer sections near the top.
- Add structured facts (hours, availability, insurance or plan tiers, inventory or appointment slots).

**Week 3: Third-party signals**

- Pick **2 or 3 review or directory sites** that matter in your field (for software: G2 or Capterra; for local services: Google, Yelp or Healthgrades, plus one relevant community).
- Answer two forum questions where you genuinely know the answer, and invite a few real customers to leave specific reviews.

**Week 4: Ship and test**

- Publish **3 short Q&A pages** (400 to 700 words each), each answering a single question.
- Run an assistant audit: ask your 10 most important buyer questions in ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, and Copilot, note where you are named or missing and which sources each one cites, and fix the pages behind the gaps. Repeat monthly with the same questions so the comparison means something.

## Measure share of voice, not just visits

In a world where many answers end without a click, the useful question is: how often is your business named when someone asks the questions that matter to you? Track it by hand with a fixed list of questions, or with tools built for it (Profound, Bluefish, and others). Report it next to calls, bookings, and leads, so the team sees visibility and revenue in one place.

## Keep it fresh

Assistants and search engines favor pages that are current. Review your most important pages at least twice a year: update prices and ranges, hours, photos, and testimonials, and add new questions as customers ask them. Put the review on a calendar so it actually happens.

## Ethics and guardrails (what not to do)

- Don't spam forums or fabricate reviews. Assistants value authentic, upvoted, question-matched threads, and fake ones get removed.
- Don't chase hacks or stuff keywords. Gray-hat tricks get neutralized over time. Build pages that deserve to be quoted.
- Don't trust anyone who promises a guaranteed spot in an AI answer. Nobody controls what an assistant says.

## Swipeable templates you can use today

**Question → best answer block (drop into any service page)**

```
### [Question your buyer actually asks]
[Best, most direct answer in 2 to 4 sentences. Include concrete numbers, timelines, or trade-offs.]

**Who it's best for:** [persona/context]
**What to do next:** [exact next step + expected time]
```

**Forum reply outline (ethical and useful)**

1. Acknowledge the asker's criteria.
2. Share the decision factors that actually matter.
3. Give a short, definite recommendation (plus when not to choose it).
4. Add one proof point (review, case result, credential).
5. Disclose your affiliation; link to a guide, not a sales page.
