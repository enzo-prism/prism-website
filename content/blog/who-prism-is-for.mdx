## Who will do the work and who will manage it?

**Hire in-house** when the work fills a continuing role, one discipline dominates, and someone can hire and support that person. A marketer inside the business has daily access to sales conversations and can change priorities quickly.

**Hire a freelancer** when the outcome is narrow and you can own the brief. A designer can deliver a strong identity; someone still has to implement it, maintain the site and verify inquiries.

**Bring in a small team** when the problem crosses design, engineering and marketing, and the owner needs one person to coordinate the work. Ask to see a decision the team made, the work that shipped and the signal the client could verify.

## Start with a week of actual work

Write down the last five growth tasks somebody at your business had to do. Beside each, write who supplied the information, who did the work and what happened after it shipped. “Update the hours at both shops” is a different job from “find out why interested shoppers rarely request a test ride.”

If similar tasks come up most days, price the role as a recurring hire. Include recruiting, management time and the tools the person will need. If the list has one well-defined deliverable, ask a freelancer for a scoped proposal with a clear handoff. If the same customer journey needs design, tracking and someone to change the site, ask a team to scope the first improvement together.

## What to ask before signing

1. **Who does the work?** Meet the person who will make the first decision and the person who can ship it. Ask what they need from you each week.
2. **What will I own?** Ask who controls the domain, site, ad accounts, source files and reporting. Find out what you can take with you and what needs ongoing maintenance.
3. **What happens when priorities change?** Give a concrete example. Your booking form breaks the week before a campaign launches. Who catches it, what work moves and who approves the change?
4. **What counts as a result?** Ask to trace one inquiry from the landing page to the person who received it. A confirmed appointment needs a booking record. A sale needs the business’s sales records.
5. **Where does the budget go?** Compare the same scope and period. Separate the team’s work from ad spend, production, software and the time your own staff will contribute.
